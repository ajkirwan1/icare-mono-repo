# Tiered Website Roadmap: Public-Facing Websites for Each Marketplace Tier

**Document Purpose**: Define the website development roadmap for each tier of the marketplace, with Tier 1 website developed in parallel with application production and completed as soon as possible.

**Document Owner**: Product & Marketing Teams
**Created**: 2026-02-01
**Last Updated**: 2026-02-01
**Status**: ACTIVE

---

## Executive Summary

The founder requires a public-facing website for each tier of the marketplace. The Tier 1 website must be developed **in parallel** with the Tier 1 application (10-12 week build) and completed **before or at the same time** as application launch.

**Key Constraints**:
- Tier 1 application build: 10-12 weeks (per tier1-build-sequence.md)
- Tier 1 website must launch alongside or before the application
- Each tier requires website updates to reflect new services/capabilities
- Introduction Agency model must be clearly communicated
- Safeguarding and trust signals critical for vulnerable adult protection

**Strategic Approach**:
- **Tier 1 Website**: Aggressive 3-4 week timeline, companionship-only messaging, pre-DBS trust signals
- **Parallel Development**: Website development runs alongside application build
- **Progressive Enhancement**: Each tier adds new pages/content aligned with expanded services
- **In-House First**: Maximize founder's direct involvement to minimize external costs and maintain control

## Tier 1 Website: Companionship-Only Launch (PRIORITY)

### Overview

**Timeline**: Weeks 1-4 (parallel to application Weeks 1-4)
**Objective**: Launch-ready website that validates companionship marketplace, builds trust, and enables user acquisition
**Service Scope**: Companionship, light housework, shopping, meal preparation ONLY
**Trust Posture**: ID verification, voluntary DBS, basic safeguarding (no mandatory DBS yet)

### Website Scope and Purpose

**Primary Goals**:
1. **Communicate Service Scope**: Clearly define Tier 1 services (companionship only, NOT personal care)
2. **Build Initial Trust**: Leverage voluntary DBS, ID verification, Introduction Agency transparency
3. **Enable Conversion**: Drive caregiver and care receiver signups
4. **Set Expectations**: Manage expectations about service limitations and roadmap
5. **Legal Compliance**: GDPR, PECR, basic safeguarding policy

**Anti-Goals** (What Tier 1 Website Does NOT Do):
- Does NOT mention personal care services (washing, dressing, toileting)
- Does NOT show DBS as mandatory (voluntary only at Tier 1)
- Does NOT mention medical conditions or care skills matching
- Does NOT promise live-in care or complex care services
- Does NOT display qualification verification (deferred to Tier 2)

### Trust Signals Possible Without Mandatory DBS

**Challenge**: Tier 1 does not require mandatory DBS (companionship is not regulated activity). How to build trust?

**Solutions**:

1. **Voluntary DBS Badge**:
   - Caregivers with existing DBS can upload for verification
   - Display "DBS Verified" badge on profiles
   - Care receivers can filter by DBS status
   - Transparent messaging: "Not required for companionship, but available"

2. **Enhanced ID Verification**:
   - Government-issued ID via Stripe Identity or equivalent
   - Photo verification
   - Address verification
   - Right to work verification

3. **Manual Admin Approval**:
   - Every profile reviewed by admin before going live
   - "Admin Approved" badge
   - Quality checks, profile authenticity

4. **Transparency About Tier Roadmap**:
   - "DBS becomes mandatory at Tier 2 (personal care services)"
   - "We're building progressively - starting safe, getting safer"

5. **Introduction Agency Messaging**:
   - "Self-employed caregivers, not employees"
   - "You choose who to hire"
   - "Platform provides tools and oversight, not care"

6. **Reviews and Ratings**:
   - Build trust through peer feedback
   - Admin moderation of reviews

7. **Safeguarding Infrastructure**:
   - 24/7 reporting system
   - Care Act 2014 compliance
   - Audit trails and incident management

### Recommended Approach: Hybrid (Option 2) with Founder Focus on Legal

**Rationale**:
1. **Speed**: 4-week timeline achievable
2. **Cost**: £4,240-£8,560 is reasonable for founder-led startup
3. **Quality**: Professional copywriter ensures trust-building messaging and accurate pricing communication
4. **Control**: Founder maintains control over website build and design
5. **Founder Focus**: Founder focuses on legal/compliance (critical) and website build (learnable skill)
6. **Brand Implementation**: Logo and branding already determined, reducing design costs

**Why Hybrid Over In-House**:
- Professional copywriter ensures safeguarding messaging is accurate and trust-building
- Founder time freed up for legal coordination and application development oversight
- Higher quality public-facing content (first impressions critical for vulnerable adult marketplace)

**Why Hybrid Over Full External**:
- Cost savings (£5,750-£10,300)
- Founder learns website building (valuable skill for future iterations)
- Faster iteration (no agency coordination delays)
- Logo and branding already determined (reduces need for external designer)

## Pricing Strategy

### Current Model (Founder Decision)

**Commission Structure**: 10% commission on agreed transactions

**Implementation Questions for Founder**:
1. **Who pays the 10% commission?**
   - Option A: Caregiver pays 10% (£15/hour booking = caregiver receives £13.50, care receiver pays £15)
   - Option B: Care receiver pays 10% service fee (£15/hour booking = caregiver receives £15, care receiver pays £16.50)
   - Option C: Split model (5% from each party)

   **Recommendation**: Option A (caregiver pays 10%) is standard for service marketplaces and simplifies care receiver pricing transparency.

2. **When is commission taken?**
   - On booking acceptance (before service delivery)
   - After booking completion (when caregiver marks complete)
   - **Recommendation**: After booking completion to reduce platform risk and refund complexity

3. **Minimum booking value or duration?**
   - Example: Minimum 2-hour booking (reduces admin overhead for small transactions)
   - **Recommendation**: 2-hour minimum for Tier 1

---

### Alternative Pricing Models to Consider

The founder is open to considering other pricing models. Below are alternatives with pros/cons for Tier 1 launch.

#### Option 1: Pure Commission Model (CURRENT DECISION)
- **Structure**: 10% commission on transactions
- **Who Pays**: Caregiver (recommended) or Care Receiver
- **Pros**:
  - Simple to understand
  - No upfront costs for caregivers (lower barrier to entry)
  - Platform only earns when value is created
  - Standard marketplace model
- **Cons**:
  - Revenue scales linearly with booking volume (slower initial revenue)
  - Requires high transaction volume to be sustainable
  - Caregivers may negotiate off-platform to avoid commission (requires safeguards)
- **Best For**: Early-stage marketplace building supply and demand

---

#### Option 2: Subscription Model (Caregiver-Focused)
- **Structure**: Monthly subscription fee for caregivers (e.g., £20-£50/month)
- **Who Pays**: Caregivers only
- **Additional Commission**: 0% (pure subscription) or low % (e.g., 3-5%)
- **Pros**:
  - Predictable monthly recurring revenue (MRR)
  - Higher revenue per caregiver if they take multiple bookings
  - Caregivers may prefer no per-transaction deduction
- **Cons**:
  - Higher barrier to entry for caregivers (upfront commitment)
  - May deter casual or part-time caregivers
  - Platform earns regardless of caregiver success (perceived as unfair if bookings are low)
  - More complex to explain than commission model
- **Best For**: Established marketplace with proven caregiver demand and booking volume
- **Tier 1 Recommendation**: NOT recommended for MVP. Too risky for caregivers who don't yet trust platform.

---

#### Option 3: Hybrid Model (Subscription + Lower Commission)
- **Structure**: Small monthly fee (e.g., £10-£15/month) + lower commission (e.g., 5%)
- **Who Pays**: Caregivers pay subscription and lower commission
- **Pros**:
  - Balances recurring revenue with transaction-based earnings
  - Lower per-booking commission (caregivers keep more per transaction)
  - Predictable baseline revenue for platform
- **Cons**:
  - More complex pricing to communicate
  - Still creates barrier to entry (monthly fee)
  - Requires calculation: "Is this better than 10% pure commission?"
- **Best For**: Post-Tier 1 optimization when caregiver ROI is proven
- **Tier 1 Recommendation**: Consider for Tier 2 or later after proving value

---

#### Option 4: Tiered Commission (Volume-Based)
- **Structure**: Commission % decreases as caregiver booking volume increases
  - Example: 10% for 0-10 bookings/month, 8% for 11-20 bookings, 5% for 21+ bookings
- **Who Pays**: Caregivers
- **Pros**:
  - Rewards high-performing caregivers (retention incentive)
  - Encourages more bookings (caregivers work toward lower commission tier)
  - Feels fair (high-volume caregivers get discount)
- **Cons**:
  - More complex to track and communicate
  - May reduce revenue from top caregivers
  - Requires booking volume analytics infrastructure
- **Best For**: Mature marketplace incentivizing caregiver loyalty and volume
- **Tier 1 Recommendation**: NOT for MVP. Too complex for launch. Consider for Tier 3+.

---

#### Option 5: Care Receiver Service Fee (Platform Pays for Verification)
- **Structure**: Caregivers list free, care receivers pay service fee per booking (e.g., £3-£5 flat fee or 5-10%)
- **Who Pays**: Care Receivers
- **Pros**:
  - No barrier to entry for caregivers (maximizes supply)
  - Care receivers pay for verification and safety services (value-aligned)
  - Platform earnings independent of caregiver rate (works for all price points)
- **Cons**:
  - May reduce care receiver demand (price sensitivity among elderly/families)
  - Perceived as additional cost on top of caregiver's rate
  - Less common in service marketplaces (harder to benchmark)
- **Best For**: High-verification platforms where families value safety checks
- **Tier 1 Recommendation**: Possible for Tier 1, but less standard than caregiver commission. Consider A/B testing post-launch.

---

#### Option 6: Freemium (Free for Basic, Paid for Premium Features)
- **Structure**: Basic listing free, premium features require subscription or higher commission
  - Example: Free listing = 10% commission, Premium listing (£20/month) = 5% commission + priority placement + profile badge
- **Who Pays**: Caregivers (choose free or premium tier)
- **Pros**:
  - No barrier to entry (free tier attracts supply)
  - Upsell opportunity for caregivers who want more visibility
  - Differentiation between casual and professional caregivers
- **Cons**:
  - Complex to communicate ("What's the difference?")
  - Risk: Most caregivers choose free tier (low revenue)
  - Requires additional features to justify premium tier
- **Best For**: High-supply marketplaces with competitive caregiver landscape
- **Tier 1 Recommendation**: NOT for MVP. Too complex and requires additional features.

## Early Adopter Incentives

### Overview

### Option 1: Reduced Commission for Early Caregivers

- **Example**: 5% commission instead of 10% for first 3 months
- **Eligibility**: First 50 caregivers to complete verification and get approved
- **Duration**: 3 months from approval date, then reverts to 10%

**Pros**:
- Strong incentive to join early (50% savings on commission)
- Builds caregiver supply quickly (critical for marketplace)
- Creates urgency ("Join before spots fill up")
- Low cost to platform (only during low-volume early phase)

**Cons**:
- Revenue sacrifice during critical growth phase
- Expectation management: Will caregivers churn when commission increases?
- Perceived unfairness to caregivers who join after limit

**Tier 1 Recommendation**: **YES - Highly Recommended**
- **Suggested Offer**: First 25 caregivers get 5% commission for 3 months
- **Messaging**: "Founding Caregiver Program - Join now for exclusive 5% commission"
- **Transition Strategy**: Email notification at 30 days and 7 days before reversion to 10%

**Structure**: First 3-6 months commission-free for early caregivers
- **Example**: 0% commission for first 100 bookings or first 6 months (whichever comes first)
- **Eligibility**: First 25-50 caregivers to complete verification

**Pros**:
- Maximum incentive to join (zero cost to caregiver)
- Extremely attractive to professional caregivers
- Builds loyal founding cohort
- Strong PR/marketing angle ("Commission-free for founding caregivers")

**Cons**:
- Zero revenue during critical early period
- High expectation: Will caregivers stay after free period ends?
- May attract caregivers only interested in free period (churn risk)
- Difficult to scale (can't offer to many caregivers without revenue impact)

**Tier 1 Recommendation**: **MAYBE - High Risk, High Reward**
- **Suggested Offer**: First 10 caregivers only, 0% commission for first 6 months
- **Positioning**: "Founding Caregiver Cohort - Help us launch in exchange for zero commission"
- **Expectation Setting**: Clear communication that this is a one-time founding offer

**Pros**:
- Viral growth mechanism (users recruit users)

- Industry-standard incentive

**Cons**:
- Requires referral tracking system (engineering effort)
- Credits reduce platform revenue per transaction
- May attract low-quality referrals if not screened

**Tier 1 Recommendation**: **YES - Low Priority for Launch, High Priority Post-Launch**
- **Launch**: Not critical for Tier 1 MVP (engineering complexity)
- **Month 3-6**: Implement after initial traction proven
- **Structure**: £20 credit for both referrer and referee after first completed booking
