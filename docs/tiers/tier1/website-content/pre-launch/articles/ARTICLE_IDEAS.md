## Article Development Priorities

#### 1. Signs Your Elderly Parent Needs More Support

**Target Word Count**: 1,800-2,200 words

**Target Keywords**:
- signs elderly parent needs help
- when does elderly parent need care
- elderly parent living alone signs
- how to tell if elderly parent needs help UK

**Content Overview**:
Signs families often miss that indicate an elderly parent needs additional support. Physical, emotional, and environmental indicators. How to assess whether they're coping or struggling. When independence is at risk. Covers subtle changes in home maintenance, personal care, nutrition, social engagement, and cognitive function.

**Key Sections**:
- Physical warning signs (weight loss, mobility decline, falls, medication management)
- Cognitive and emotional signs (confusion, memory issues, mood changes)
- Environmental signs (home condition, mail piling up, unpaid bills)
- Social and behavioural changes (withdrawal, isolation, unusual behaviours)
- What to do if you notice these signs
- How to have the conversation

**Why This Matters**: Families often struggle to know when "normal ageing" crosses into needing help. This article provides clarity and permission to act.

**Related CTAs**: Newsletter subscription, waitlist for companionship finding when launched

#### 2. How to Talk to Your Elderly Parent About Needing Help

**Target Word Count**: 1,600-2,000 words

**Target Keywords**:
- talking to elderly parent about care
- how to convince elderly parent to accept help
- elderly parent refuses help
- having difficult conversations with ageing parents UK

**Content Overview**:
Practical guidance for having sensitive conversations about accepting support. Addresses common resistance ("I'm fine"), fear of losing independence, and family dynamics. Provides specific conversation starters, what to avoid, and how to involve parents in decision-making.

**Key Sections**:
- Why these conversations are so difficult (for both sides)
- Preparing for the conversation (timing, setting, who should be present)
- Conversation frameworks and specific phrases
- Common objections and how to respond
- When to involve siblings or other family
- What to do if they refuse help
- Building trust and revisiting over time

**Why This Matters**: This is often the biggest barrier families face. Many delay getting help because they don't know how to raise the topic without causing offence or upset.

**Related CTAs**: Newsletter for ongoing guidance, waitlist

#### 3. What Is Companion Care? A Complete Guide for UK Families

**Target Word Count**: 1,800-2,200 words

**Target Keywords**:
- what is companion care UK
- companion care vs personal care
- companionship services for elderly
- what does a companion carer do

**Content Overview**:
Comprehensive explanation of companion care - what it is, what it isn't, who it's for, and how it differs from personal care or nursing. Clarifies terminology, explains typical activities, discusses costs (general range, not iCare pricing), and helps families understand whether it's appropriate for their situation.

**Key Sections**:
- Definition of companion care
- What companion carers do (and don't do)
- Difference between companionship, personal care, and nursing
- Who benefits from companion care
- How to find and choose a companion carer
- What to expect during visits
- Questions to ask potential companions
- Typical costs and arrangements (industry general, not iCare-specific)

**Why This Matters**: Many families don't understand what companion care is or that it's an option. This article educates and positions companionship as a legitimate, valuable service.

**Related CTAs**: Strong waitlist CTA as this is high commercial intent

#### 6. Social Isolation vs Loneliness: Understanding the Difference

**Target Word Count**: 1,400-1,800 words

**Target Keywords**:
- social isolation vs loneliness
- difference between isolation and loneliness elderly
- elderly isolation UK

**Content Overview**:
Clarifies the difference between social isolation (objective lack of contact) and loneliness (subjective feeling of being alone). Explains that someone can be isolated but not lonely, or surrounded by people but still lonely. Helps families understand what their loved one is experiencing.

**Key Sections**:
- Defining social isolation
- Defining loneliness
- Why they're different and why it matters
- Can you have one without the other?
- Which is your loved one experiencing?
- Different interventions for each
- Why companionship addresses both

**Why This Matters**: Understanding the distinction helps families identify the right solutions. Builds on flagship loneliness article.

**Related CTAs**: Newsletter, waitlist

## SEO and Distribution Strategy

### On-Page SEO:
- All articles optimised for target keywords
- Strong meta descriptions
- Internal linking between related articles
- Clear heading hierarchy (H1, H2, H3)
- Image alt text
- URL structure: icare.uk/care-guidance/[article-slug]

### Distribution Channels:
- **Newsletter**: Excerpt and link to full article
- **Social media**: LinkedIn (professional angle), Facebook (family communities)
- **Medium/Substack**: Republish excerpts with link back
- **Local Age UK partnerships**: Offer content for their newsletters
- **Care forums and Facebook groups**: Share where appropriate and allowed
- **Pinterest**: Visual formats for activity ideas, safety tips

### Measuring Success:
- Organic search traffic
- Newsletter signups from articles
- Waitlist signups attributed to content
- Time on page and engagement
- Social shares
- Inbound links

## Content Quality Standards

Every article must meet these standards:

**Tone**: Warm, empathetic, professional, non-clinical, accessible
**Reading Level**: Age 12-14 (Flesch-Kincaid 7-8)
**Length**: Substantive (1,500+ words minimum for SEO)
**Evidence**: Cite credible sources (NHS, Age UK, academic research)
**Structure**: Clear sections, scannable, bullet points and lists
**Accuracy**: No medical advice, no regulatory claims, no unsubstantiated promises
**Value**: Genuinely useful, not just marketing
**CTAs**: Appropriate and non-pushy (newsletter primary, waitlist secondary)
