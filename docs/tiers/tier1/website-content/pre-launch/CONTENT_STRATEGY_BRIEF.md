# Pre-Launch Website Content Strategy Brief

**Document Purpose**: Foundational guidance for all pre-launch website content creation. This document defines audiences, messaging, tone, and constraints that all content must follow.

**Document Owner**: Product Director
**Created**: 2026-02-01
**Status**: ACTIVE - Canonical Reference for Content Creation

---

## Executive Summary

The pre-launch website exists to accomplish three objectives:

1. **Build Awareness** - Establish iCare as a trusted voice in elderly companionship care
2. **Capture Demand** - Convert visitors into waitlist signups (high intent) and newsletter subscribers (lower intent)
3. **Build Trust** - Position iCare as safe, thoughtful, and different from typical platforms

This is a **marketing website**, not an operational platform. We are selling a vision and building a community before the product exists. Every word must serve these goals while respecting strict constraints on what we cannot yet communicate.

## 1. Target Audiences

### Primary Audience: Families Seeking Care

#### Persona: Sarah, 52, The Concerned Daughter

**Demographics**:
- Age: 45-65
- Location: UK (suburban and urban)
- Relationship: Adult child of elderly parent
- Income: Middle to upper-middle class
- Tech comfort: Moderate (uses smartphone, shops online, some social media)

**Situation**:
Sarah's mother, Margaret (78), lives alone since her father passed away two years ago. Margaret is physically capable but increasingly isolated. She's stopped going to her book club, rarely sees friends, and seems flat when Sarah visits on Sundays. Sarah works full-time and lives 45 minutes away. She feels guilty that she can't visit more often.

**Pain Points**:
- Guilt about not being able to do more
- Worry about mum being lonely and declining
- Uncertainty about what help is actually needed
- Fear of suggesting "care" and offending mum's independence
- Overwhelmed by care agency options (too clinical, too expensive, too impersonal)
- Distrust of strangers entering mum's home
- Time pressure - can't spend hours researching options

**Emotional State**:
- Anxious and worried
- Guilty and stretched thin
- Hopeful but sceptical
- Protective of parent's dignity
- Seeking permission to get help

**What She Wants**:
- Someone trustworthy to spend time with mum
- Peace of mind that mum is safe and not alone
- Flexibility (not a big commitment upfront)
- To feel like a good daughter
- Simple, clear information (not jargon)

**What She Fears**:
- Strangers taking advantage of vulnerable mum
- Mum feeling like a burden or "put in care"
- Making the wrong choice
- Complicated contracts and commitments
- Judgement from others

**How She Found Us**:
- Google search: "companionship for elderly parent UK"
- Facebook group recommendation
- Article shared by friend
- Local community post

**Decision Factors**:
- Trust signals (verification, safety)
- Testimonials and social proof
- Ease of understanding
- Flexibility and control
- Emotional resonance with messaging

## 4. Tone and Voice Guidelines

### Overall Voice Character

iCare speaks like a **knowledgeable, warm friend who works in elderly care** - someone who understands the challenges personally, speaks plainly, and genuinely wants to help. Not a corporation. Not a startup. A trusted ally.

### Voice Attributes

| Attribute | Description | Dial Position |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Warm** | Empathetic, human, caring | High |
| **Professional** | Credible, competent, trustworthy | Medium-High |
| **Honest** | Direct, transparent, no hype | High |
| **Hopeful** | Optimistic, forward-looking | Medium |
| **Simple** | Clear, accessible, jargon-free | High |
| **Respectful** | Dignified, never patronising | High |

### Tone by Context

| Context | Tone Adjustment |
|---------|-----------------|
| **Homepage hero** | Warm, emotionally resonant, inviting |
| **Problem description** | Empathetic, validating, serious but not alarming |
| **How it works** | Clear, encouraging, practical |
| **About us** | Personal, passionate, authentic |
| **Safety content** | Reassuring, confident, specific |
| **Caregiver content** | Respectful, empowering, collegial |
| **CTAs** | Inviting, low-pressure, clear value |
| **Legal/Privacy** | Plain English, transparent, accessible |

---

### Tone Examples: Do and Don't

#### Headline Examples

| Don't | Do | Why |
|-------|-----|-----|
| "The UK's #1 Care Platform" | "Safe companionship for elderly adults across the UK" | We can't claim #1; focus on what we offer |
| "Revolutionising elderly care" | "A better way to find trusted companionship" | Avoid startup hyperbole; be specific |
| "Find care in minutes!" | "Find the right person, not just any person" | We're about quality, not speed |
| "Uber for elderly care" | "Connecting families with caring companions" | Avoid tech comparisons; we're human-first |

#### Body Copy Examples

| Don't | Do | Why |
|-------|-----|-----|
| "Our cutting-edge platform leverages..." | "We're building a simple way to..." | Avoid tech jargon |
| "Studies show that loneliness kills" | "Loneliness affects health as much as smoking 15 cigarettes a day" | Be specific and factual, not sensational |
| "Don't let your parent suffer alone" | "Your parent deserves company, and you deserve peace of mind" | Positive framing, not guilt |
| "Caregivers are the backbone of society" | "Caregiving is skilled, meaningful work that deserves respect" | Specific and direct, not cliche |

#### CTA Examples

| Don't | Do | Why |
|-------|-----|-----|
| "Sign up now!" | "Join the waitlist" | Accurate to pre-launch state |
| "Don't miss out!" | "Be among the first" | Invitation, not pressure |
| "Get started for free" | "Join our founding community" | We're not launching yet; build belonging |
| "Subscribe to our newsletter" | "Get care guidance in your inbox" | Benefit-focused |

---

### Reading Level and Accessibility

**Target Reading Level**: Age 12-14 (Flesch-Kincaid Grade 7-8)

**Guidelines**:
- Sentences: Average 15-20 words (max 25)
- Paragraphs: 2-4 sentences
- Words: Prefer common words over technical terms
- Structure: Clear headings, short paragraphs, bullet points for lists
- Avoid: Jargon, acronyms without explanation, complex sentence structures

**Accessibility Considerations**:
- Write for screen readers (clear heading hierarchy)
- Avoid idioms that don't translate well
- Don't rely on colour alone to convey meaning
- Provide text alternatives for any visual concepts

## 6. CTA Strategy

### Two Primary CTAs

| CTA Type | Purpose | Audience | Commitment Level |
|----------|---------|----------|------------------|
| **Join the Waitlist** | Capture high-intent users who want to use the platform | Both | High |
| **Subscribe to Care Guidance** | Capture lower-intent users interested in content | Both | Low |

### When to Use Each CTA

#### Use "Join the Waitlist" When:

- End of Homepage hero section
- End of How It Works pages
- End of About Us
- End of Safety Commitment
- Inline within problem/solution narrative
- Primary CTA position (most prominent)

**Waitlist CTA Copy Variants**:
- "Join the Waitlist" (standard)
- "Join Our Founding Community" (belonging emphasis)
- "Be Among the First" (exclusivity emphasis)
- "Get Early Access" (benefit emphasis)
- "Join the Waitlist for Families" (segmented)
- "Join the Caregiver Waitlist" (segmented)

**Waitlist Value Proposition**:
"Join [X] others waiting for a better way to find trusted companionship care. You'll get:
- Priority access when we launch
- Exclusive updates on our progress
- The chance to shape what we build"

#### Use "Subscribe to Care Guidance" When:

- Care Guidance hub page
- End of articles
- Secondary CTA position (less prominent)
- Footer (all pages)
- For visitors not ready to commit to waitlist

**Newsletter CTA Copy Variants**:
- "Subscribe to Care Guidance" (standard)
- "Get Care Guidance in Your Inbox" (benefit emphasis)
- "Join Our Weekly Newsletter" (frequency clarity)
- "Stay Informed" (simple)

**Newsletter Value Proposition**:
"Practical advice for families navigating elderly care. Weekly insights on:
- Understanding your loved one's needs
- Having difficult conversations
- Finding the right support
- Research and resources"

---

### CTA Design Principles

1. **One primary CTA per viewport** - Don't overwhelm with choices
2. **Clear value exchange** - What do they get for signing up?
3. **Low friction** - Minimal fields (email + role for waitlist)
4. **No false urgency** - Invitation, not pressure
5. **Segmentation where valuable** - Families vs caregivers on waitlist
6. **Confirmation and next steps** - Always acknowledge the action

## 7. Content Length Guidelines

### By Page Type

| Page Type | Word Count | Rationale |
|-----------|------------|-----------|
| Homepage | 1,400-1,800 | Comprehensive but scannable; multiple sections |
| About Us | 1,200-1,700 | Story-driven; needs space for narrative |
| How It Works | 1,000-1,400 | Clear explanation; not overwhelming |
| Care Guidance Hub | 400-600 | Index page; points to content |
| Articles | 1,500-2,400 | Substantive thought leadership |
| FAQ | 800-1,200 | Concise Q&A format |
| Safety Commitment | 800-1,200 | Thorough but focused |
| Contact | 300-500 | Minimal; functional |
| Privacy Policy | 500-800 | Minimal for email capture |
| Waitlist Confirmation | 300-500 | Brief; action-focused |

### Section Length Guidelines

| Section Type | Sentences | Words | Notes |
|--------------|-----------|-------|-------|
| Hero headline | 1 | 8-15 | Punchy, memorable |
| Hero subheadline | 1-2 | 20-40 | Expands on headline |
| Intro paragraph | 2-4 | 40-80 | Sets context |
| Body paragraph | 3-5 | 60-120 | One idea per paragraph |
| Feature/benefit point | 2-3 | 30-60 | Concise and specific |
| CTA section | 2-3 | 40-80 | Value prop + action |

### Formatting Guidelines

- **Headings**: Every 200-300 words maximum
- **Paragraphs**: 2-4 sentences; break up long paragraphs
- **Lists**: Use for 3+ related items; easier to scan
- **Bold**: Use sparingly for key terms; not whole sentences
- **Links**: Descriptive text, not "click here"

## 9. What NOT to Communicate

### Absolute Restrictions

The following topics must NOT appear anywhere on the pre-launch website:

#### Pricing and Business Model

| Do Not Mention | Why |
|----------------|-----|
| Commission rates (e.g., "10%") | Business model not finalised; competitive sensitivity |
| Platform fees | Same as above |
| Caregiver earnings projections | Cannot guarantee; legal risk |
| "Free for families" | May imply hidden costs elsewhere |
| Specific price ranges | Market not validated |
| Payment processing details | Operational detail |
| How caregivers get paid | Operational detail |

**If asked about pricing**: "We're focused on making great care accessible and fairly compensating caregivers. Details will be available when we launch."

#### Verification and Safety Details

| Do Not Mention | Why |
|----------------|-----|
| DBS checks (specifically) | Verification approach not finalised |
| "All caregivers background checked" | Cannot claim until operational |
| Specific verification steps | Operational detail |
| ID verification methods | Operational detail |
| Right to work checks | Regulatory/operational detail |
| Admin approval process | Operational detail |

**If asked about safety**: "Safety is our foundation. We're building thorough verification into everything we do. More details when we launch."

#### Regulatory and Legal Status

| Do Not Mention | Why |
|----------------|-----|
| CQC (Care Quality Commission) | Don't discuss regulatory status |
| "Regulated" or "unregulated" | Legal positioning |
| Introduction Agency model | Business model term |
| Care Act compliance | Legal claim |
| Self-employment status | Employment law complexity |
| Insurance requirements | Operational detail |

#### Launch Timeline

| Do Not Mention | Why |
|----------------|-----|
| Specific launch dates | May change; creates expectation |
| "Launching in [month]" | Same as above |
| Specific milestone dates | Same as above |

**Acceptable language**: "Coming soon", "In the coming months", "We're working hard to launch soon"

#### Operational Details

| Do Not Mention | Why |
|----------------|-----|
| How booking works (specifically) | Not built yet |
| Cancellation policies | Operational detail |
| Messaging features | Not built yet |
| App features | Not built yet |
| Geographic coverage specifics | Not determined |
| Service area limits | Operational detail |

---

### Topics to Handle Carefully

These topics can be mentioned but require careful framing:

#### Personal Care Services

**Current Position**: We're launching with companionship only.

**Acceptable**: "We're starting with companionship services. Personal care support is on our roadmap."

**Avoid**: Detailed timelines, promises about when personal care will be available.

#### Live-In Care

**Current Position**: Not available at launch.

**Acceptable**: "We're beginning with regular visits. Live-in care is something we're considering for the future."

**Avoid**: Commitments or timelines.

#### Geographic Coverage

**Current Position**: UK-wide ambition, but supply may be limited initially.

**Acceptable**: "We're launching across the UK."

**Avoid**: Specific regions, city-level promises about caregiver availability.

#### What Makes Us Different

**Acceptable**: Values-based differentiation (trust, flexibility, respect, quality).

**Avoid**: Direct competitor comparisons, claims about being "better" or "cheaper".

## 10. Content Quality Checklist

Before any content is considered complete, verify against this checklist:

### Message Alignment

- [ ] Addresses correct target audience (families, caregivers, or both)
- [ ] Leads with higher-priority messages
- [ ] Emotional journey is appropriate (recognition, hope, trust, action)
- [ ] Key messages are present and clear

### Tone and Voice

- [ ] Warm and empathetic, not corporate
- [ ] Professional but not clinical
- [ ] Honest and transparent
- [ ] Hopeful without hype
