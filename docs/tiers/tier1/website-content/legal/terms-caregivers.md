## 4. KEY SELF-EMPLOYMENT INDICIA **[LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED - IR35 CRITICAL]**

The following provisions establish genuine self-employment status:

### 4.1 No Mutuality of Obligation

**Platform has NO obligation to**:
- Offer you bookings
- Guarantee minimum work or income
- Provide continuous work

**You have NO obligation to**:
- Accept any booking request
- Accept any minimum number of bookings
- Be available at any specific times
- Remain registered on the Platform

This is a **zero-commitment relationship**. Either party may cease the relationship at any time without consequence.

**Reference**: Pimlico Plumbers Ltd v Smith [2018] UKSC 29 (lack of mutuality supports self-employment)

### 4.2 Control Over Work Performance

**You have COMPLETE control over**:
- How you deliver care services (methods, techniques, approach)
- What equipment, tools, or materials you use
- What route you take to reach bookings
- What clothing you wear (no mandatory uniform)
- Whether you work for other platforms or clients

**The Platform does NOT**:
- Supervise your care delivery
- Dictate care delivery methods
- Require you to follow specific procedures or scripts
- Monitor your work performance during bookings
- Discipline you for care quality (only for platform policy violations)

**Reference**: Uber BV v Aslam [2021] UKSC 5 (excessive control indicates employment)

### 4.3 Substitution Rights **[LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED - IR35 CRITICAL]**

**You have the RIGHT to send a SUBSTITUTE** to fulfill bookings on your behalf, subject to:
- The substitute being appropriately qualified and experienced
- The substitute completing Platform verification (ID, right to work, DBS if applicable)
- The care receiver's consent (care receiver may decline substitute)

This is a key indicator of genuine self-employment. If you cannot personally attend a booking, you may arrange a verified substitute instead of cancelling.

The Platform will provide a substitution request feature to facilitate this right.

**Reference**: Pimlico Plumbers Ltd v Smith [2018] UKSC 29 (substitution supports self-employment)

### 4.4 Rate Setting Autonomy

**You set your own hourly rate** within Platform guidance ranges. The Platform does NOT:
- Mandate your rate
- Set uniform pricing
- Require you to match competitor rates

You may:
- Change your rate at any time (applies to new bookings only)
- Charge different rates for different services within Tier 1 scope
- Decline bookings you deem insufficiently compensated

### 4.5 Availability Control

**You control your own availability**. You may:
- Set your availability calendar as you choose
- Update availability at any time
- Block out dates for holidays, other work, or personal reasons
- Work limited or extensive hours as suits your business

The Platform does NOT require minimum availability or working hours.

### 4.6 Right to Decline Bookings

**You have ABSOLUTE DISCRETION** to accept or decline any booking request. You may decline for any reason (or no reason), including:
- You are unavailable
- The rate is too low
- The location is too far
- You prefer not to work with that care receiver
- You have other commitments

**No penalties apply for declining bookings**. Your profile visibility is NOT affected by declining bookings.

**Reference**: Addison Lee Ltd v Gascoigne [2018] UKEAT 0289_17_0103 (mandatory acceptance indicates employment)

### 4.7 Financial Risk

You bear financial risk as a business, including:
- Cost of insurance
- Cost of travel to bookings
- Cost of training and professional development
- Risk of non-payment if care receiver disputes booking (subject to Platform dispute resolution)
- Opportunity cost of accepting one booking over another

This financial risk differentiates self-employment from employment.
