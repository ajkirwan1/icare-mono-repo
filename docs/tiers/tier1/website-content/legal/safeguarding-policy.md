## 2. Legal and Regulatory Framework **[LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED]**

### 2.1 Care Act 2014

The Care Act 2014 places duties on local authorities and partner organizations to:
- Protect adults at risk of abuse or neglect
- Make or arrange enquiries when safeguarding concerns arise
- Establish Safeguarding Adults Boards (SABs)
- Cooperate with safeguarding investigations

**Section 42 Duty**: Local authorities must make enquiries if they reasonably suspect an adult:
- Has needs for care and support
- Is experiencing, or at risk of, abuse or neglect
- As a result of those needs is unable to protect themselves

**Platform Role**: While we are NOT a local authority, we operate as an Introduction Agency serving vulnerable adults. We have a duty to:
- Recognize and report safeguarding concerns
- Cooperate with SAB investigations
- Implement safeguarding policies and procedures
- Train staff on safeguarding

**Reference**: Care Act 2014, Sections 42-46

### 2.2 Other Relevant Legislation

- **Mental Capacity Act 2005**: Capacity presumption, best interests, Lasting Power of Attorney
- **Safeguarding Vulnerable Groups Act 2006**: DBS disclosure requirements (mandatory at Tier 2+)
- **Human Rights Act 1998**: Right to life, prohibition of torture, right to respect for private life
- **Equality Act 2010**: Non-discrimination, reasonable adjustments
- **Data Protection Act 2018 / UK GDPR**: Lawful sharing of information for safeguarding

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 What is Safeguarding?

**Safeguarding** means protecting adults' right to live in safety, free from abuse and neglect.

### 3.2 Who is a Vulnerable Adult?

A **vulnerable adult** (under Care Act 2014) is an adult who:
- Has needs for care and support (whether or not the local authority is meeting those needs), AND
- Is experiencing, or at risk of, abuse or neglect, AND
- As a result of those needs is unable to protect themselves

Many of our care receivers meet this definition due to age, disability, or care needs.

### 3.3 Types of Abuse

The Care Act 2014 identifies the following types of abuse:

**1. Physical Abuse**:
- Hitting, slapping, pushing, kicking, restraint, rough handling
- Misuse of medication
- Inappropriate physical contact

**2. Emotional/Psychological Abuse**:
- Verbal abuse, threats, intimidation, humiliation
- Controlling or coercive behavior
- Isolation from family or friends
- Denial of choices or dignity

**3. Sexual Abuse**:
- Non-consensual sexual contact or acts
- Sexual harassment or innuendo
- Indecent exposure
- Forcing someone to watch sexual acts or pornography

**4. Financial or Material Abuse**:
- Theft, fraud, or exploitation
- Misuse of property, possessions, or benefits
- Pressure to change a will or financial arrangements
- Requesting off-platform payments to avoid safeguards

**5. Neglect and Acts of Omission**:
- Failure to provide care, assistance, or support
- Failure to provide access to healthcare, medication, or nutrition
- Failure to maintain hygiene or living conditions
- Abandonment

**6. Discriminatory Abuse**:
- Abuse based on protected characteristics (age, disability, gender, race, religion, sexual orientation)
- Harassment, slurs, or denial of services

**7. Domestic Abuse**:
- Abuse from a family member or household member
- Physical, emotional, financial, or sexual abuse
- Controlling or coercive behavior

**8. Modern Slavery**:
- Human trafficking, forced labor, domestic servitude
- (Less likely in our context but must be recognized)

**9. Institutional Abuse**:
- Abuse within an organizational or care setting
- (Platform-level safeguarding failures would constitute institutional abuse)

**10. Self-Neglect**:
- Neglect of personal hygiene, health, or living conditions
- Refusal of care or support
- Hoarding

**Reference**: Care Act 2014 Statutory Guidance, Section 14.17

## 4. Six Safeguarding Principles (Care Act 2014)

Our safeguarding approach is guided by the six principles set out in the Care Act 2014:

**1. Empowerment**: People being supported and encouraged to make their own decisions and give informed consent.
- We provide information to help care receivers make informed choices about caregivers
- We respect care receiver autonomy in booking decisions

**2. Prevention**: It is better to take action before harm occurs.
- We verify caregiver identity, right to work, and background checks
- We monitor messages for safeguarding keywords
- We provide safeguarding training to caregivers

**3. Proportionality**: The least intrusive response appropriate to the risk presented.
- We escalate concerns proportionately (internal investigation → SAB referral)
- We balance safeguarding with individual freedom and choice

**4. Protection**: Support and representation for those in greatest need.
- We provide 24/7 safeguarding reporting mechanisms
- We prioritize vulnerable adults in investigations
- We escalate to authorities when necessary

**5. Partnership**: Local solutions through services working together.
- We cooperate with Safeguarding Adults Boards
- We share information lawfully with authorities
- We work with family members and professionals

**6. Accountability**: Accountability and transparency in safeguarding practice.
- We maintain audit trails of safeguarding reports and actions
- We review incidents and learn from them
- We are transparent about our safeguarding approach

**Reference**: Care Act 2014 Statutory Guidance, Section 14.13

## 5. Our Safeguarding Responsibilities

### 5.1 Platform Responsibilities

As an Introduction Agency, we are responsible for:

**Verification and Quality Signals**:
- Verifying caregiver identity (government-issued ID)
- Verifying right to work in the UK
- Verifying DBS certificates (voluntary at Tier 1, mandatory at Tier 2+)

**Safeguarding Infrastructure**:
- Providing safeguarding reporting tools (in-app, email, phone)
- Monitoring messages for safeguarding keywords
- Investigating safeguarding reports
- Escalating concerns to Safeguarding Adults Boards when appropriate
- Maintaining audit trails of incidents

**Training and Guidance**:
- Providing safeguarding guidance to caregivers
- Training admin staff on safeguarding recognition and response
- Publishing this Safeguarding Policy
- Providing resources on recognizing abuse

**Cooperation with Authorities**:
- Reporting concerns to local authority SABs
- Cooperating with safeguarding investigations
- Sharing information lawfully (GDPR-compliant)
- Responding to requests from CQC or law enforcement

### 5.2 What We Do NOT Do

The Platform does NOT:
- Provide care services (caregivers are self-employed, not our employees)
- Control how care is delivered
- Supervise caregivers during care delivery
- Conduct formal care assessments or risk assessments
- Replace statutory safeguarding duties of local authorities

**We are an Introduction Agency with safeguarding duties, NOT a care provider with clinical governance responsibilities.**

**Reference**: Health and Social Care Act 2008 (CQC registration), Care Act 2014

### 5.3 Caregiver Responsibilities

Caregivers using the Platform have a duty to:
- **Recognize signs of abuse or neglect** and report concerns immediately
- **Treat care receivers with dignity and respect**
- **Maintain professional boundaries** (no financial exploitation, no inappropriate conduct)
- **Report safeguarding concerns** via Platform reporting tools
- **Cooperate with investigations** if a concern is raised
- **Call 999 in emergencies** if there is immediate danger

### 5.4 Care Receiver/Family Responsibilities

- **Report concerns about caregivers** immediately via Platform reporting tools
- **Provide accurate information** about care needs and emergency contacts
- **Cooperate with investigations** if a concern is raised
- **Call 999 in emergencies** if there is immediate danger

## 6. How to Recognize Abuse

### 6.1 Signs and Indicators of Abuse

**Physical Abuse**:
- Unexplained bruises, cuts, burns, or injuries
- Injuries inconsistent with explanation given
- Fear or anxiety around a particular person
- Reluctance to undress or be examined

**Emotional/Psychological Abuse**:
- Withdrawal, depression, anxiety
- Changes in behavior or demeanor
- Low self-esteem, self-blame
- Fear of speaking in front of others

**Sexual Abuse**:
- Unexplained sexually transmitted infections
- Torn, stained, or bloody underclothing
- Fear of certain people or places
- Inappropriate sexual behavior or knowledge

**Financial Abuse**:
- Unexplained lack of money or inability to pay bills
- Missing personal possessions or valuables
- Sudden changes to will or financial arrangements
- Requests for off-platform payments

**Neglect**:
- Poor hygiene, dirty clothing, or strong odor
- Malnutrition, dehydration, or weight loss
- Untreated medical conditions
- Unsafe living conditions (hoarding, lack of heating)

**Discriminatory Abuse**:
- Withdrawal from activities
- Fear or anxiety around certain groups
- Denial of services based on protected characteristics

**Domestic Abuse**:
- Controlling behavior from family members
- Isolation from friends or services
- Unexplained injuries
- Fear of going home

**Self-Neglect**:
- Severe lack of self-care (hygiene, nutrition)
- Hoarding or squalor
- Refusal of care or support
- Deteriorating health

### 6.2 Contextual Factors

Consider the following when assessing concerns:
- Is the adult at risk a vulnerable adult (care needs, unable to protect themselves)?
- Is there a pattern of incidents or a single serious incident?
- Is there an imbalance of power (caregiver-care receiver relationship)?
- Does the adult lack mental capacity to consent or understand?
- Is there evidence of coercion or control?

**Reference**: Care Act 2014 Statutory Guidance, Section 14.17-14.97

## 7. How to Report a Safeguarding Concern

### 7.1 Reporting Options

**If there is IMMEDIATE DANGER**:
- **Call 999** (Police/Ambulance)
- **Then** report via Platform so we can support and document

**If there is NO immediate danger**:

**For Caregivers and Care Receivers**:
- **In-App Reporting**: Use "Report Safeguarding Concern" feature
- **Email**: [SAFEGUARDING_EMAIL]
- **Phone**: [SAFEGUARDING_PHONE] (24/7 response for urgent concerns)

**For Public/External Reporters**:
- **Email**: [SAFEGUARDING_EMAIL]
- **Phone**: [CONTACT_PHONE]

### 7.2 What to Include in a Report

Please provide as much detail as possible:
- **Who**: Name of person at risk, caregiver involved (if applicable)
- **What**: Description of the concern (what happened, when, where)
- **When**: Date and time of incident(s)
- **Where**: Location of incident
- **Evidence**: Any photos, messages, or documents supporting the concern
- **Immediate risk**: Is the person currently at risk?
- **Your contact details**: So we can follow up (anonymous reports accepted but harder to investigate)

### 7.3 Anonymous Reporting

You may report anonymously, but this may limit our ability to:
- Gather further information
- Provide updates on outcome
- Protect the reporter from retaliation

We encourage you to provide contact details, which will be kept confidential.

### 7.4 Confidentiality

Safeguarding reports are treated as **confidential** and shared only with:
- Platform safeguarding team
- Local authority Safeguarding Adults Board (if escalated)
- Police or other authorities (if required)
- Care receiver or family (with care receiver's consent, if appropriate)

We will NOT share your report with the alleged perpetrator unless legally required.

**Reference**: Data Protection Act 2018, Section 10(3) (safeguarding exemption)

## 8. Our Response to Safeguarding Concerns

### 8.1 Immediate Response (Within 24 Hours)

**1. Acknowledgment**:
- We acknowledge receipt of your report within 24 hours (for non-urgent) or immediately (for urgent)
- We assign a safeguarding case reference number

**2. Initial Triage**:
- We assess the severity and immediacy of risk
- We determine whether emergency escalation is required

**3. Immediate Safeguarding Actions** (if necessary):
- Suspend caregiver account pending investigation
- Block contact between parties
- Notify care receiver/family if at risk
- Escalate to authorities if immediate danger

### 8.2 Investigation (Within 7 Days for Non-Critical)

**1. Evidence Gathering**:
- Review messages, booking history, and previous reports
- Interview reporter (if contact details provided)
- Interview care receiver (if safe and appropriate)
- Interview caregiver (unless this creates risk)
- Collect evidence (photos, documents, witness statements)

**2. Assessment**:
- Determine whether abuse or neglect has occurred
- Assess level of risk to care receiver
- Determine whether Section 42 criteria are met (SAB referral required)

**3. Decision**:
- **Substantiated**: Evidence supports the concern
- **Unsubstantiated**: No evidence to support the concern
- **Inconclusive**: Insufficient evidence but concern remains

### 8.3 Escalation to Safeguarding Adults Board **[LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED]**

We will escalate to the local authority Safeguarding Adults Board (SAB) if:

**Section 42 Criteria Met**:
- The adult has needs for care and support (or appears to)
- The adult is experiencing, or at risk of, abuse or neglect
- The adult is unable to protect themselves as a result of their care needs

**Other Escalation Triggers**:
- Serious physical harm or sexual abuse
- Financial exploitation exceeding £1,000
- Multiple incidents suggesting pattern of abuse
- Criminal activity suspected (we also notify police)
- Care receiver lacks mental capacity and is at risk
- Institutional abuse or systemic safeguarding failure

**Reference**: Care Act 2014, Section 42

### 8.4 SAB Liaison Process

**1. Referral**:
- We contact the local authority SAB for the area where the adult lives
- We provide a written referral using SAB's referral form (if available)
- We include all relevant information (name, address, nature of concern, evidence)

**2. Cooperation**:
- We cooperate fully with SAB enquiries
- We share information lawfully (GDPR safeguarding exemption)
- We provide access to Platform records (messages, bookings, verification)

**3. Outcome**:
- SAB determines whether a safeguarding enquiry is needed
- SAB may take over the case or ask us to continue monitoring
- We implement any recommendations from SAB

**Find Your Local SAB**: https://www.gov.uk/report-abuse-of-older-person

### 8.5 Police Notification

We will notify police if:
- A crime has been committed (assault, theft, fraud, sexual assault)
- There is immediate danger to life
- The care receiver requests police involvement
- SAB advises us to notify police

**Emergency**: 999
**Non-Emergency**: 101
**Action Fraud** (financial crimes): 0300 123 2040

### 8.6 Outcomes and Actions

Following investigation and any SAB/police involvement, we may take the following actions:

**Against Caregivers**:
- **Warning**: First-time minor policy violation
- **Suspension**: Pending investigation or retraining
- **Termination**: Serious safeguarding breach, substantiated abuse
- **Ban**: Permanent removal from Platform, reported to authorities

**Against Care Receivers** (if care receiver is perpetrator):
- **Warning**: Inappropriate conduct toward caregiver
- **Suspension**: Pending investigation
- **Termination**: Harassment, abuse, or safeguarding breach

**Support to Care Receivers**:
- Referral to local authority adult social care
- Referral to independent advocacy services
- Connection to victim support services
- Assistance with alternative care arrangements

**Platform Improvements**:
- Update policies or procedures
- Enhanced verification requirements
- Additional caregiver training
- System improvements to prevent recurrence

### 8.7 Notification to Reporter

We will notify the reporter of the outcome (if contact details provided):
- Investigation findings (substantiated, unsubstantiated, inconclusive)
- Actions taken (to extent we can disclose without breaching confidentiality)
- Any further steps the reporter should take

**Timeline**: Within 28 days of initial report (or sooner for urgent cases)

## 9. Safeguarding for Specific Situations

### 9.1 Mental Capacity and Consent **[LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED]**

**Mental Capacity Act 2005 Principles**:
- **Assume capacity**: Presume person has capacity unless proven otherwise
- **Support decision-making**: Help person understand and make decisions
- **Right to unwise decisions**: Capacity is decision-specific
- **Best interests**: Decisions for those lacking capacity must be in best interests
- **Least restrictive**: Choose option that restricts freedom least

**If care receiver lacks mental capacity**:
- Family member or Lasting Power of Attorney (LPA) may make decisions
- We assess whether booking arrangements are in care receiver's best interests
- We escalate if concerned about abuse or exploitation by family member

**Deprivation of Liberty Safeguards (DoLS)**: Live-in care (Tier 3+) may engage DoLS if care receiver lacks capacity and is restricted. We provide guidance but do NOT authorize DoLS (local authority role).

**Reference**: Mental Capacity Act 2005

### 9.2 Domestic Abuse

If we suspect domestic abuse by a family member:
- We do NOT disclose report to family member (risk of escalation)
- We escalate to SAB and domestic abuse services
- We offer support to care receiver discreetly

**National Domestic Abuse Helpline**: 0808 2000 247 (24/7)

### 9.3 Self-Neglect

If a caregiver reports signs of self-neglect:
- We do NOT suspend care services (care receiver needs support)
- We escalate to local authority adult social care for assessment
- We work with SAB to develop safeguarding plan

**Reference**: Care Act 2014, Section 42(2) (self-neglect included)

### 9.4 Off-Platform Contact and Financial Exploitation

**Risk**: Care receivers may be pressured to pay caregivers directly (off-platform) to avoid platform safeguards and commission.

**Prevention**:
- Terms of Service prohibit off-platform payments
- Message monitoring flags financial exploitation keywords
- "Report Off-Platform Payment Request" feature

**Response**:
- Investigate immediately
- Suspend caregiver account
- Escalate to police if fraud suspected
- Refund care receiver if payment made

### 9.5 Caregiver Safeguarding

Caregivers may also be at risk (e.g., aggressive care receiver, unsafe working conditions).

Caregivers should report concerns about:
- Aggressive or abusive care receivers
- Unsafe working conditions
- Requests for inappropriate services
- Harassment or sexual advances

We will investigate and may suspend care receiver account if safeguarding breach confirmed.

## 11. Information Sharing and Confidentiality **[LEGAL REVIEW REQUIRED]**

### 11.1 Lawful Information Sharing

We may share personal data for safeguarding purposes under:
- **UK GDPR Article 6(1)(d)**: Vital interests (life-threatening situations)
- **UK GDPR Article 6(1)(e)**: Public interest (safeguarding duties)
- **UK GDPR Article 9(2)(i)**: Special category data for safeguarding (if health data involved at Tier 2+)
- **Data Protection Act 2018, Section 10(3)**: Safeguarding exemption from normal restrictions

We share information **proportionately** and only with:
- Safeguarding Adults Boards
- Police or law enforcement
- Health or social care professionals (with consent or in best interests)
- Courts (if court order issued)

**Reference**: Data Protection Act 2018, UK GDPR

### 11.2 Confidentiality Limits

We explain to all users that:
- Safeguarding reports may be shared with authorities
- Confidentiality cannot be guaranteed if risk of harm
- We have a duty to report concerns even without consent (if in person's best interests)
